Infectious Diseases

How this theme is defined and matched

Broad proxy: studies whose title and abstract were coded to a Global Burden of Disease (GBD 2023) infectious cause: HIV/AIDS and sexually transmitted infections, respiratory infections and tuberculosis, enteric infections, malaria and neglected tropical diseases, other infectious diseases, or an infection of unstated type. Studies without a disease code are not treated as evidence of absence. Wellcome programme scope ↗

How publications were matched: by the GBD 2023 cause coded from each title and abstract (the condition the evaluated intervention targets). A publication counts if it has any of these GBD groups:
HIV/AIDS and sexually transmitted infectionsRespiratory infections and tuberculosisEnteric infectionsNeglected tropical diseases and malariaOther infectious diseasesInfections, disease not stated
GBD codes were assigned by AI from titles and abstracts under the team’s coding rules and checked against a second coder and MeSH headings; they were not reviewed publication by publication. "Infections, disease not stated" is the team’s group for infections where the specific disease is not reported.

How HPV vaccine and hepatitis C studies are classified. GBD counts each illness once, under the disease people suffer or die from rather than under its cause, so totals never double-count. HPV, for example, is counted under cervical cancer, just as smoking-related lung cancer is counted as lung cancer. The map follows the same rule: each study is coded to the condition its intervention aims to prevent or treat. So HPV vaccine studies are coded to cervical cancer (Neoplasms), hepatitis C treatment to cirrhosis and other chronic liver diseases, and maternal Group B strep vaccination to neonatal disorders. These classifications follow GBD's rules. Because this theme is defined by GBD infectious causes, and these conditions are not among them, such studies are reported separately rather than counted in the theme. The same rule applies to every publication in the map, whoever funds it; no publication was moved in or out of the theme by hand.

Climate & Health

How this theme is defined and matched

Reviewed keyword screen: the titles and abstracts of all eligible studies were searched for climate, extreme heat and weather, air pollution, emissions and related terms, and every candidate was reviewed against Wellcome's Climate & Health framing (health impacts of climate change, climate-sensitive infectious disease, air pollution, and mitigation with health co-benefits). Studies of health care's own emissions are included and shown separately. The GBD cause list has no climate category, so this theme does not use disease codes. Wellcome programme scope ↗

How publications were matched: no GBD code. The GBD cause list classifies diseases and injuries; it has no climate category. Climate affects health through many causes at once (heat, air pollution, infections, injuries), so no set of GBD causes identifies climate-and-health research. Instead, every title and abstract was searched for these keywords:
  • Health impacts of climate change: climate change, global warming, climate crisis, climate-sensitive, climate adaptation or resilience, extreme heat, heatwaves, heat stress, extreme weather, floods, drought, wildfires, cyclones and hurricanes, sea-level rise, natural disasters
  • Air pollution: air pollution, air quality, particulate matter (PM2.5, PM10), black carbon, household or indoor air, cookstoves, solid or biomass fuels
  • Mitigation with health co-benefits: greenhouse gas, carbon or CO2 emissions, carbon footprint, carbon tax or pricing, decarbonisation, net zero, low carbon, sustainable diets, planetary health, environmental sustainability, active travel, clean or renewable energy
  • Climate-sensitive infectious disease: a climate or weather term (climate, weather, rainfall, seasonal temperature) together with dengue, malaria, cholera, vector-borne disease, Zika, chikungunya, leishmaniasis, schistosomiasis or diarrhoeal disease
  • Health-sector emissions: an emissions term together with a health-care setting (hospitals, surgery, inhalers, anaesthesia, dialysis, telemedicine)
Excluded before matching: non-environmental uses such as 'organisational climate', 'current climate', 'positron emission' or 'flooding with fluid'.

✓ Human-verified: each of the 138 candidates was reviewed by hand against Wellcome’s Climate & Health framing; 102 were included and 36 excluded, with the reason recorded for each (exports/climate_health_review_decisions_2026-10-12.csv).

Why so few publications? This map covers health economic evaluations (cost-effectiveness and related studies of health interventions) and reviews of them. Most climate-and-health research is epidemiology or impact modelling, which this map does not cover. 102 of 41,610 eligible publications (0.2%) address climate. The 138 candidates found by the keyword screen were each reviewed, and a broader search for related terms found almost no further publications. The small number reflects how little economic evaluation has been published in this area so far, rather than gaps in the search.

Technical details and sources

Each funder is counted once per eligible publication. A publication can name multiple funders and belong to multiple themes, so counts do not sum across funders or themes. Funders come from Crossref publication metadata; this map screened titles and abstracts only. “No funder identified” means none was found there, not that the publication reports no funding: many acknowledge funders only in the full text. Funding is recorded for 26.8% of the evidence base, so absence of a name does not establish absence of support. Burroughs Wellcome Fund is a separate organisation and is excluded from the Wellcome cohort.

Setting comparisons use single-country publications with known income, taking the World Bank group in force on 1 January of each publication's year; all other publications stay visible in the multi-country, regional or income-unavailable category. Names are lightly normalised (NIHR, HTA, NIH, MRC, Gates and common variants); agencies and their programmes are not comprehensively merged. Rankings count identified funders, not funding amounts, and tied funders share a rank. A programme named in an acknowledgement does not establish membership in a current priority programme.

Infectious Diseases and Mental Health use the Global Burden of Disease (GBD 2023) causes coded from each publication's title and abstract (the condition the evaluated intervention targets); 36,446 of 41,610 eligible publications have a disease code, so publications without one can still contain relevant evidence. Climate & Health uses a keyword screen of all titles and abstracts (scripts/build_climate_candidates.py), after which every candidate was reviewed against Wellcome's Climate & Health framing; decisions and their basis are in exports/climate_health_review_decisions_2026-10-12.csv. A broader recall check found almost no further publications. Classification uses titles and abstracts, not full texts. Wellcome-funded publications beyond the three themes are grouped by keywords in their titles (first matching group wins) to describe what they cover; this grouping does not change any theme count. These definitions do not establish current Wellcome grant eligibility or early-intervention status.

Theme mapping. Infectious Diseases: any of the GBD 2023 groups HIV/AIDS and sexually transmitted infections; Respiratory infections and tuberculosis; Enteric infections; Neglected tropical diseases and malaria; Other infectious diseases; Infections, disease not stated. Mental Health covers all mental health: Mental disorders; Substance use disorders, because Wellcome also funds mental health work beyond its stated focus. Wellcome’s stated focus, anxiety, depression and psychosis (Wellcome: Mental Health), is reported as a subset using the GBD 2023 level-3 causes Depressive disorders; Anxiety disorders; Schizophrenia; Bipolar disorder (1,092 publications); bipolar disorder is included because Wellcome’s funding calls list it among psychotic disorders (example call). GBD codes were assigned by AI from titles and abstracts under the team’s coding rules (see methods/gbd_coding/GBD_CODING.md in the repository) and checked against a second coder and MeSH headings; they were not reviewed publication by publication. How HPV vaccine and hepatitis C studies are classified: GBD counts each illness once, under the disease people suffer or die from rather than under its cause, so totals never double-count. HPV, for example, is counted under cervical cancer, just as smoking-related lung cancer is counted as lung cancer. The map follows the same rule: each study is coded to the condition its intervention aims to prevent or treat. So HPV vaccine studies are coded to cervical cancer (Neoplasms), hepatitis C treatment to cirrhosis and other chronic liver diseases, and maternal Group B strep vaccination to neonatal disorders. These classifications follow GBD's rules. Because this theme is defined by GBD infectious causes, and these conditions are not among them, such studies are reported separately rather than counted in the theme. The same rule applies to every publication in the map, whoever funds it; no publication was moved in or out of the theme by hand.Climate & Health has no GBD mapping: The GBD cause list classifies diseases and injuries; it has no climate category. Climate affects health through many causes at once (heat, air pollution, infections, injuries), so no set of GBD causes identifies climate-and-health research.
