Explore individual themes

Wellcome's infectious disease focus areas: tuberculosis, cholera, vector-borne diseases (such as schistosomiasis, leishmaniasis and dengue) and drug-resistant infections. They are represented by the GBD 2023 causes tuberculosis; diarrhoeal diseases, the closest GBD code for cholera, which has no code of its own; and the vector-borne causes malaria, dengue, leishmaniasis, schistosomiasis, Chagas disease, lymphatic filariasis, onchocerciasis, Zika, yellow fever and African trypanosomiasis. Drug-resistant infection is not a separate GBD category; drug-resistant tuberculosis, for example, is included under tuberculosis. Other infectious diseases, such as HIV/AIDS, lower respiratory infections and COVID-19, are not included.

Technical note: how publications were matched to Wellcome's priorities

How were publications matched to Wellcome's priorities?

We matched publications to Wellcome's specified priorities using GBD 2023 disease codes, the classification used across the HEE evidence map. Within Infectious Diseases, the priorities are tuberculosis, cholera, vector-borne diseases and drug-resistant infections; within Mental Health, anxiety, depression and psychosis. The same criteria are applied to every publication, whoever funds it, so results are comparable across funders.

For each priority we used the closest available GBD codes:

  • Most priorities have a direct GBD code: tuberculosis, anxiety, depression, and the vector-borne diseases on WHO's list.
  • Where GBD has no specific code, we used the nearest category: diarrhoeal diseases for cholera, and schizophrenia and bipolar disorder for psychosis, following Wellcome's own list of psychotic disorders.
  • Drug-resistant infections and early intervention are not disease categories in GBD, so they are covered through the underlying conditions rather than identified separately.

Because GBD classifies diseases rather than research areas, the mapping is an indicative guide to Wellcome's priorities rather than an exact match. We set out its known limitations below and in the table.

Scope of the mapping

  • Publications on other conditions are outside these priorities. Examples are other infectious diseases, such as HIV/AIDS, lower respiratory infections and COVID-19 (5,056 publications, 56 Wellcome-funded), and other mental health conditions, including substance use.
  • Some GBD codes combine eligible and other conditions: encephalitis, other neglected tropical diseases, broad infection groups, and mental disorders with no specific condition (200 publications). These are not included, to keep the mapping specific.
  • Climate & Health, which was identified by keywords rather than GBD codes, is not included, and neither are publications identified through additional screening. This keeps every funder's publications identified in the same way.

A publication outside the mapping may still contribute to Wellcome's priorities, for example through cross-cutting research.

Counting and funding

  • Each publication is counted once, even when it is in both themes (1 is).
  • Funders are taken from Crossref publication metadata. A funder is identified for 26.8% of all 41,610 publications; “funder not identified” does not mean unfunded.
  • Wellcome-funded means Wellcome Trust is among the identified funders, alone or with others (Burroughs Wellcome Fund is separate).
  • Publications can report several funders, so funder counts do not add up to theme totals. Tied funders share a rank.
  • Single-country publications are grouped by the World Bank income group in their publication year; multi-country, regional and unclassified publications form a third group.
  • Publications from 2010 to 3 August 2026; 2026 is partial.
  • Counts describe economic evidence (including reviews) and the funders it acknowledges. They do not measure funding amounts, grant portfolios or strategic performance.
