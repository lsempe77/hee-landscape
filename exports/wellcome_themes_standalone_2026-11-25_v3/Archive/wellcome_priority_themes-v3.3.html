Explore individual themes

Wellcome's infectious disease focus areas: tuberculosis, cholera, vector-borne diseases (such as schistosomiasis, leishmaniasis and dengue) and drug-resistant infections. They are represented by the GBD 2023 causes tuberculosis; diarrhoeal diseases, a broad stand-in for cholera, which has no separate GBD cause; and the vector-borne causes malaria, dengue, leishmaniasis, schistosomiasis, Chagas disease, lymphatic filariasis, onchocerciasis, Zika, yellow fever and African trypanosomiasis. Drug-resistant infection is not a GBD cause and cannot be identified from these codes. Other infectious diseases, such as HIV/AIDS, lower respiratory infections and COVID-19, are not included.

    TuberculosisDiarrheal diseasesMalariaDengueLeishmaniasisSchistosomiasisChagas diseaseLymphatic filariasisOnchocerciasisZika virusYellow feverAfrican trypanosomiasis
  • Rationale: Wellcome's Infectious Disease page: “Our focus includes: Tuberculosis (TB)…; Cholera…; Vector-borne diseases such as schistosomiasis, leishmaniasis and dengue…; Drug-resistant infections…” (Wellcome, Infectious Disease, accessed 27 Nov 2026). Diarrhoeal diseases is a broad stand-in for cholera, which has no GBD cause. Vector-borne diseases is mapped as a category: Wellcome names examples (“such as”), so every vector-borne GBD cause is included.
  • Limitations: diarrhoeal diseases also covers non-cholera infections such as rotavirus and Shigella. Vector-borne causes are those in the WHO vector-borne diseases fact sheet; Diseases WHO lists as vector-borne but which have no GBD cause of their own (for example Japanese and tick-borne encephalitis, West Nile fever, chikungunya, Rift Valley fever, Crimean-Congo haemorrhagic fever, plague, scrub typhus and Lyme disease) sit in mixed GBD codes (encephalitis, other neglected tropical diseases, other unspecified infectious diseases) and are not captured: about 35 publications by title search, 1 of them Wellcome-funded (Japanese encephalitis in Bangladesh). Drug-resistant infection cannot be identified from GBD codes. 5,056 publications with other infectious causes (56 Wellcome-funded) are not included; 665 of them are coded only to a broad infection group that cannot be assigned to an eligible cause.
  • GBD codes were assigned from titles and abstracts under the team's coding rules and checked against a second coder and MeSH headings; they were not reviewed publication by publication. Full methodology

The thematic analysis provides an indicative characterisation of the HEE evidence base using existing GBD classifications as a proxy for Wellcome's specified research priorities. Differences in the scope and granularity of these classification systems limit the extent to which thematic alignment can be captured.

Wellcome-funded publications not captured by this mapping may nevertheless contribute to Wellcome's research priorities, including through cross-cutting research and areas not represented by the selected GBD categories. The results should therefore not be interpreted as a comprehensive assessment of Wellcome's research portfolio or its alignment with strategic priorities.

Scope exclusions

  • Other infectious causes (e.g. HIV/AIDS, lower respiratory infections, COVID-19, rabies, intestinal nematodes): Counted in the broad Infectious Diseases theme in v5/v6; outside Wellcome's focus areas, so not eligible here.
  • Infections coded only at a broad level ('Respiratory infections and tuberculosis', 'Enteric infections', 'Neglected tropical diseases and malaria', 'Infections, disease not stated'): Cannot be assigned to a specific eligible cause; not eligible. Flagged for review.
  • Other mental disorders and substance use disorders: Previously counted in the broad Mental Health theme (v5/v6); outside Wellcome's specified focus, so not eligible here.
  • 'Mental disorders' with no level-3 condition coded: Cannot be assigned to anxiety, depression or psychosis; not eligible. Flagged for review.
  • Climate & Health: Identified by a reviewed keyword screen, not GBD codes; excluded from every displayed analysis and aggregate.
  • 17 Wellcome-funded publications added on title-and-abstract review (v6): Identified only by supplementary screening of Wellcome-funded publications; excluded.

5,056 publications with other infectious causes (56 Wellcome-funded) and 200 publications coded only to “Mental disorders” (1 Wellcome-funded) are among those not eligible.

Why a publication may not be captured

Non-matching may reflect GBD classification limitations, cross-cutting research, the exclusion of Climate & Health, the exclusion of publications identified only through supplementary screening, or research in other areas. These reasons have not been systematically assessed; non-matching does not imply that a publication lacks thematic relevance.

Verification versus supplementary identification

Publications that meet the GBD criteria are included whether or not their coding was also checked by hand. Publications found only through supplementary searches or manual screening, such as the 17 Wellcome-funded publications added on review in the previous version, are excluded, so every funder's publications are identified in the same way.

Unit of analysis and deduplication

The unit is a unique eligible publication (one record ID), not a grant, funding amount or funder mention. Combined figures count each publication once even when it is captured by both themes (1 publications, 0 of them Wellcome-funded). Breakdown groups can overlap and are labelled where they do.

Funding identification and missingness

Funders come from Crossref publication metadata; titles and abstracts were screened, not full texts. A funder is identified for 26.8% of all 41,610 publications (11,149). “Funder not identified” means none was found in the metadata, not that the publication was unfunded. A publication is Wellcome-funded when Wellcome Trust is among its identified funders, alone or with others (Burroughs Wellcome Fund is a separate funder). Funder names are lightly normalised (NIHR, HTA, NIH, MRC, Gates and common variants) and counted once per publication.

Multiple funders and rankings

A publication may report several funders and counts towards each, so funder counts do not sum to the theme total. Ranks use competition ranking: tied funders share a rank. Rankings compare publication counts, not funding amounts.

Country income groups

Single-country publications use the World Bank income group in force on 1 January of the publication year. Multi-country, regional and global publications, and single-country publications whose income group is unavailable, form a third group.

Publication years

Publications from 2010 to 3 August 2026, when searches ended; 2026 is therefore partial. The annual trend uses a fixed 0–100 axis with ticks every 30, so that year-to-year differences are not visually exaggerated; exact counts are given on hover.

What publication counts can and cannot show

Publication counts describe the economic evidence in the map and the funders it acknowledges. They do not measure funding amounts, expenditure, grant portfolios or strategic performance, and the map covers economic evidence (including reviews) rather than all research Wellcome funds.
